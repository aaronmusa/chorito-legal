Chorito

Privacy Policy

Effective 15 November 2026

Chorito keeps your household’s chores on a server I run, so everyone in the household sees the same thing. It stores only what the app needs to work, shares it only with the people in your household, and never sells it or uses it for advertising. There are no ads and no analytics.

Who runs it

Chorito is made by Aaron Musa, an independent developer. Your data is stored with Supabase, which hosts Chorito’s database, sign-in and photo storage on my behalf. Supabase processes it only to run the service for me. If you sign up with email, the confirmation and password reset emails are sent through Google’s Gmail, which sees the address they go to.

What Chorito stores

  • Your account — the name you confirm, plus how you sign in. With Apple: a user ID from Apple and the email address Apple shares with the app (which can be Apple’s private relay address if you choose to hide yours). With email: your email address and your password, which is stored only as a one-way hash, never as the password itself.
  • Your household — its name, timezone and invite code, who belongs to it, and each member’s role (manager, co-manager or member).
  • Chores and what happens to them — chore names, notes, schedules, points, who each one is assigned to, when it was marked done, review decisions and their reasons, requests to take, swap or reschedule a chore, points gained and lost, and the monthly standings.
  • Photos — up to five photos you add, from your camera or photo library, when you mark a chore done. A chore can ask for at least one as proof.
  • Comments, mentions and nudges — what you write on chores, and reminders people send each other.
  • Reports and blocks — if you report a comment or a chore’s photos and note: what you reported, the reason you picked, and when. If you block someone: who. A report is shown to the household’s managers without your name, and emailed to me with your name so I can look into it. A block is never shown to the person you blocked.
  • Notifications — a record of what you were told about and whether you’ve read it, plus the two notification settings you can change.
  • A push token — the address Apple gives the app so it can send notifications to your iPhone. It identifies the app on your device, not you.

Who can see it

The members of your household see each other’s names, chores, photos, comments, points and decisions. That is the point of the app. Managers and co-managers also see requests waiting on them and can approve or reject work. Nobody outside your household can see any of it; the database itself enforces this, not just the app.

If you belong to more than one household, each household sees only what happens inside it.

Crash reports

Chorito uses Firebase Crashlytics, from Google, to tell me when the app crashes so I can fix it. A crash report contains the technical details of the crash, your device model and iOS version, the app version, and a random identifier for this installation of the app. It does not contain your name, email, household or chores.

What Chorito does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising or tracking SDKs.
  • No analytics beyond crash reports.
  • No selling or renting your data, and no sharing it with anyone except the services above that run Chorito.
  • No tracking you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • No location, contacts, microphone or health data.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept while you have an account. If you leave a household, its history keeps the chores you did and the points you earned, because the household’s past months and year board would otherwise change under everyone else; your access to it ends.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from the app’s settings, or by emailing me at j.aaronmusa@gmail.com from the address on your account. Deleting it removes your account, your profile and your push token, revokes Chorito’s access to your Apple ID if you signed in with Apple, and takes you out of every household. Chores and points you recorded, and proof photos you took for them, stay in each household’s history without your name attached.

Children

Chorito is meant to be set up by adults. Children can be members of a household that an adult manages, but the app is not directed at children under 13 and I do not knowingly collect their personal information outside a household an adult set up.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new effective date. If a change affects what Chorito stores or who can see it, the app will tell you before it applies.
